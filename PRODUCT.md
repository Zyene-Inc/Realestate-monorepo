# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Coach Johnson Realty serves four connected audiences. Public visitors compare available homes and decide whether the company feels trustworthy. Tenants need fast access to rent, lease, maintenance, documents, messages, and account details. Approved agent companies submit listings and manage buyer inquiries. Internal rental, sales, and super-admin staff coordinate approvals, properties, leases, payments, vendors, communications, and reporting.

People often use the public and tenant surfaces on a phone, while agents and staff rely on wider desktop and tablet workflows. Every surface must make the next action obvious without hiding important context.

## Product Purpose

The product joins public property discovery with role-specific operations for sales and rental management. Success means visitors can find and inquire about a home with confidence, tenants can complete common tasks without calling the office, agents can move listings through review cleanly, and staff can understand operational state at a glance.

## Brand Personality

Grounded, precise, neighborly. The experience should communicate steady stewardship, capable service, and respect for the people who live in and care for each property. Copy is direct and specific. Confidence comes from clarity and follow-through, not luxury language.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI landing pages with glowing gradients, fake dashboards, vague claims, and oversized slogans.
- Navy-and-gold luxury real-estate templates that feel interchangeable or exclusionary.
- Decorative glassmorphism, excessive card grids, and motion that delays routine work.
- Startup-style names, AI terminology, robot icons, fake metrics, or invented customer proof.
- Dense admin screens that depend on color alone, hide actions, or break on small screens.

## Design Principles

1. Make the property and the task the focal point. Decoration never competes with homes, balances, requests, or approvals.
2. Earn trust through specificity. Use concrete labels, honest status language, clear ownership, and visible next steps.
3. Design one connected service. Public, tenant, agent, and staff surfaces share a coherent visual vocabulary while respecting different density needs.
4. Adapt structure, not just size. Mobile prioritizes one clear action at a time; tablet and desktop reveal more context without changing meaning.
5. Use motion as feedback. Interactions respond immediately, transitions explain continuity, and reduced-motion alternatives remain complete.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA across public and authenticated surfaces. All actions must be keyboard accessible with visible focus, semantic labels, 44px minimum touch targets, sufficient contrast, meaningful status text, and screen-reader-friendly forms and tables. Respect reduced motion, reduced transparency, system color preference, 200% text zoom, and narrow 375px viewports without horizontal page scrolling.
